In this chapter we describe the techniques used to coherently controlling a nuclear spin that is strongly coupled to an \Er ion. 

\section{Driving a nuclear spin-1/2}
\labsec{driving_nuclear_spin_half}

If one wishes to drive the state of a nuclear spin, the most straightforward way is to apply a radiofrequency (rf) pulse resonant with a nuclear spin transition. However, as detailed in \refsec{rabi_oscillations_theory}, in our setup the superconducting resonator acts as a narrow band-pass filter centered around $\omega_r\approx7.7$~GHz. Consequently, all rf drives applied to the sample will be reflected from the resonator and direct driving of a nuclear spin becomes impossible.

\subsection{Sideband driving}

In order to find an alternative driving scheme, we now consider the Hamiltonian of an \Er spin-1/2 coupled via hyperfine interaction to a nuclear spin-1/2 while under a magnetic field $B_0$. As described in \refch{electron_nuclear_interaction}, the Hamiltonian under the secular approximation can be written as

\begin{equation}
    \cH{} = \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x.
\end{equation}

This Hamiltonian is analytically solvable \sidecite{schweiger_principles_2001} and the nuclear and electron transition frequencies are given by

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/energy_levels_half.pdf}
    \caption[Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2]{Energy level diagram of an \Er coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2. Because the electron and nuclear gyromagnetic factors have opposite signs ($\omega_I < 0 < \omega_S$) the ground state has an antiparallel configuration.}
    \labfig{energy_levels_half}
\end{marginfigure}

\begin{align}
\begin{split}   
     \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)} &= \left(\omega_I + \frac{A_\parallel}{2}\right) \cos \eta_\alpha - \frac{A_\perp}{2} \sin \eta_\alpha \approx \omega_I + \frac{A_\parallel}{2}, \\ 
    \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)} &= \left(\omega_I - \frac{A_\parallel}{2}\right) \cos \eta_\beta - \frac{A_\perp}{2} \sin \eta_\beta \approx \omega_I - \frac{A_\parallel}{2}, \\ \\
    \omega_S^{(\uparrow)} &= \omega_S + \frac{1}{2} \left( \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)} - \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)} \right) \approx \omega_S + \frac{A_\parallel}{2}, \\ 
    \omega_S^{(\downarrow)} &= \omega_S - \frac{1}{2} \left( \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)} - \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)} \right) \approx \omega_S - \frac{A_\parallel}{2} \\ \\
    \eta_{\alpha, \beta} &= \arctan{\left(\frac{-A_\perp}{A_\parallel \pm 2\omega_I}\right)} \approx \mp \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I} \ll 1.
\end{split}    
\end{align}

where $\eta_{\alpha, \beta}$ are the mixing angles, and we have taken the high-field regime approximation, $|\omega_I| \gg |A_\parallel|, |A_\perp|$. 

In the high field regime, we consider as a perturbation all terms that do not commute with $S_z$ and $I_z$ in the Hamiltonian, that is, the perpendicular hyperfine term $A_\perp S_zI_x$.

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
        \mathcal{H} &= \mathcal{H}_0+V, \\
        \mathcal{H}_0 &= \omega_S\cdot S_z + \omega_I\cdot I_z + A_\parallel S_z I_z,  \\
         V & = A_\perp S_z I_x.
\end{split}
\end{align}

The eigenstates of $\mathcal{H}_0$ correspond to the uncoupled spin states $\ket{\Downarrow \uparrow}$, $\ket{\Downarrow \downarrow}$, $\ket{\Uparrow \uparrow}$, $\ket{\Uparrow \downarrow}$, as illustrated in \reffig{energy_levels_half}. At first order, the perturbed states will be

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \ket{\psi_{m_S,\uparrow}} \approx \ket{m_S, \uparrow} - \frac{m_S}{2}\frac{A_\perp}{\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S} \ket{m_S, \downarrow}, \\
    \ket{\psi_{m_S,\downarrow}} \approx \ket{m_S, \downarrow} + \frac{m_S}{2}\frac{A_\perp}{\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S} \ket{m_S, \uparrow}, \\
\end{split}
\end{align}

with $m_S\in\{\uparrow\,\equiv1/2 ,\; \downarrow\,\equiv-1/2\}$ being the electron magnetic number. This results in a weak admixture of the nuclear spin states. In particular, the nuclear wavefunctions are different depending on the state of the electron spin, as the perturbation term is proportional to $m_S$, which result in the following non-zero matrix elements

\begin{equation}
    \bra{\Uparrow \downarrow} S_x \ket{\Downarrow \uparrow} \approx - \bra{\Uparrow \downarrow} S_x \ket{\Downarrow \uparrow} \approx \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I} \ll 1.
\end{equation}


Consequently, these \textit{a priori} forbidden transitions become weakly allowed. They involve simultaneous flips of the electron and nuclear spins and are referred to as zero-quantum (ZQ) and double-quantum (DQ) transitions\sidenote{The names indicate the net change in the total magnetic quantum number: in a ZQ transition the electron and nuclear spins flip in opposite directions, while for a DQ transition they flip in the same direction.} or red and blue sidebands. The frequency of these transitions is given by

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \omega_\text{ZQ} = |\omega_S^{(\downarrow)}| - |\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}| \approx \omega_S - \omega_I, \\
    \omega_\text{DQ} = |\omega_S^{(\uparrow)}|   + |\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}| \approx \omega_S + \omega_I,
\end{split}
\end{align}

which are close to the frequency of the electron spin transitions. When the resonator is tuned to the electron spin, the ZQ and DQ transitions can be driven through a microwave tone.
