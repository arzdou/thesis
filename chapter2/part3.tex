\section{Driving a \W nuclear spin through the \Er spin}
\labsec{driving_nuclear_spin_half}

If one wishes to drive the state of a nuclear spin, the most straightforward way is to apply a radiofrequency (rf) pulse resonant with the allowed NMR transitions. However, the superconducting resonator in our setup acts as a narrow band-pass filter centered around $\omega_r\approx7.7$~GHz, as detailed in \refsec{spin_coupled_to_resonator}. Consequently, all rf drives applied to the sample will be reflected from the resonator and direct driving of a nuclear spin is impossible in our setup. We now proceed to discuss two different methods that can be used to drive a nuclear spin using only microwave pulses

%In ensemble EPR, the resonance lines are inhomogeneously broadened, and a strong microwave tone will simultaneously drive the EPR-allowed transitions of some spins and the ZQ or DQ transitions of others. The resulting EPR spectrum shows a central hole as well as several side-peaks, which can be used to determine hyperfine coupling parameters. This method recieves the name of electron-electron double resonance (ELDOR)-detected NMR (EDNMR)~\sidecite{schosseler_pulsed_1994}. In this section, we discuss the single-spin version of the EDNMR sequence.

\subsection{Zero- and double-quantum transitions}

The \Er spin is resonant with the cavity when the magnitude of the magnetic field along the $c$-axis is $B_0\approx0.45$~T. At this field, the Larmor frequency of \W is  $\omega_I\approx800$~kHz. The ZQ and DQ transitions are detuned from the EPR-allowed transitions by this frequency, and can be therefore driven with a microwave tone (see \reffig{energy_levels_half}b). As these transitions simultaneously change the state of both spins, resonant driving of the ZQ and DQ transitions is a valid strategy to control the coupled nuclear spin.

%In Chapter \ref{ch:detection_and_control_of_individual_w} we demonstrate how to control the state of the nuclear spin by resonantly driving these transitions. 

We now proceed to derive the Rabi rate of ZQ and DQ transitions when resonantly driven. We begin by recalling the spin Hamiltonian in the high-field regime derived in \refsec{w_er_hamiltonian}:

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/energy_levels_half.pdf}
    \caption[Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2]{\textbf{a.} Energy level diagram of an \Er coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2. Electron spin transitions are marked with a black arrow while zero and double quantum transitions are marked with blue and red arrows respectively.
    \textbf{b.} Schematic of the transitions, superimposed with the resonator response shown as a gray Lorentzian of FWHM$\kappa$. Allowed transitions (black peaks) are quasi-resonant with $\omega_r$, forbidden transitions (red and blue) are detuned by $\sim \omega_I$}
    \labfig{energy_levels_half}
\end{marginfigure}

\begin{equation}
    \cH{}/\hbar = \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x.
\end{equation}

As seen in \refsec{w_er_hamiltonian}, this Hamiltonian defines a four level system, shown in \reffig{energy_levels_half}a. The energy levels are given by

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \ket{1} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}     + \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}\right) , \\
    \ket{2} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}   - \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}\right), \\
    \ket{3} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Downarrow\uparrow}   - \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Downarrow\downarrow}\right) , \\
    \ket{4} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Downarrow\downarrow} + \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Downarrow\uparrow}\right),
\end{split}
\end{align}


where $\mathcal{N}$ is a normalization constant. The eigenstates therefore correspond to the uncoupled product states with a small admixture of the opposite nuclear spin component. This mixing produces a small, but nonzero, $\hat{S}_x$ matrix elements:

\begin{equation}
    \bra{1} \hat{S}_x \ket{4} \approx - \bra{2} \hat{S}_x \ket{3} \approx \frac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ll 1.
    \label{eq:zq_dq_matrix_elements}
\end{equation}

Therefore, the zero- and double-quantum transitions $\ket{1}\leftrightarrow\ket{4}$ and $\ket{2}\leftrightarrow\ket{3}$, which are strictly forbidden in the absence of mixing, become weakly allowed with oscillator strength on the order of $A_\perp/(4\omega_I)$, much smaller than that of the allowed EPR transitions:

\begin{equation}
    \bra{1} \hat{S}_x \ket{3} \approx \bra{2} \hat{S}_x \ket{4} \approx 1/2,
\end{equation}

The Rabi rate of an allowed EPR transition with a resonant the cavity is given in \refsec{spin_coupled_to_resonator} by $\Omega = 4g_0 \epsilon_d / \kappa$, where $\epsilon_d$ is the applied microwave amplitude. To obtain the ZQ and DQ Rabi rates at the \textit{same} drive amplitude we simply rescale $\Omega$ by the ratio of the relevant matrix elements. Including the cavity filtering (the sideband is detuned from the cavity by $\sim\omega_I$) gives the final ZQ/DQ Rabi rate

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\text{ZQ}} \approx \Omega_{\text{DQ}} \approx \Omega \cdot \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4\omega_I^2/\kappa^2}}.
\end{equation}

Because the matrix elements and the cavity filtering are nearly the same for the ZQ and DQ sidebands, their driven Rabi rates are essentially equal. This equation will be used in the analysis of the experimental results to extract the value of $A_\perp$.

We note that sideband driving necessarily involves populating the electron spin excited state, so the operation is limited by the much faster electron dephasing time $T_2^*$ rather than by the long nuclear coherence. Consequently, electron spin decoherence rate imposes a fundamental limit on the fidelity of nuclear-spin gates implemented via sideband drives.

\subsection{AC-Zeeman shifts}
\labsec{ac-zeeman_shifts}

%We now introduce the concept of AC-Zeeman shifts, also known as the magnetic AC-Stark shift. 
When driving a two-level system off-resonance the energy levels are renormalized and the transition frequency is shifted with the square of the amplitude of the drive and is proportional to the matrix element of the transition. A derivation of this effect requires second order perturbation theory on the time-dependent Hamiltonian and can be found in ref.~\sidecite{cohen-tannoudji_atom_2024}. 

When a microwave pulse with amplitude\sidenote{From here onwards, we characterize the amplitude of a microwave pulse by the equivalent Rabi frequency on resonance} $\Omega$ and frequency $\omega_d = \omega_S + \Delta$ is applied on a single electron spin, the transition frequency is shifted by

\begin{equation}
    \delta\omega_\mathrm{AC} = -\frac{\Omega^2}{2\Delta},
    \label{eq:simple_ac_zeeman}
\end{equation}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/ac_zeeman_diagram.pdf}
    \caption[AC Zeeman shift diagram]{AC Zeeman shift diagram. Driving the ZQ transition induces AC-Zeeman shifts on the electron spin transitions ($\delta\omega^{(\uparrow)}_\mathrm{AC}$, $\delta\omega^{(\downarrow)}_\mathrm{AC}$), which modify the resonance condition for the sideband transition.}
    \labfig{ac_zeeman_diagram}
\end{marginfigure}

which results in a smaller frequency when $\Delta>0$ and \textit{vice versa}. 

We now address the AC-Zeeman shifts occurring when driving the forbidden ZQ or DQ transitions. As described in \refsec{w_er_hamiltonian}, there are six different transitions in this system, two EPR-allowed, two NMR-allowed and the ZQ and DQ transitions. Under an off resonant drive, \textit{all} the transitions are renormalized following Eq.~\ref{eq:simple_ac_zeeman}. The total shift of the ZQ and DQ transitions can be calculated by independently considering the effect of the drive to each transition:

\begin{itemize}    
    \item Both EPR-allowed transitions are shifted by the presence of the off-resonant drive by
    \begin{equation}
        \delta\omega^{\uparrow}_\mathrm{AC} = -\frac{\Omega^2} {2\Delta - A_\parallel}, \quad | \quad \delta\omega^{\downarrow}_\mathrm{AC} = -\frac{\Omega^2} {2\Delta + A_\parallel},
    \end{equation}
    \item The AC-Zeeman shift is proportional to the matrix element of the transition \cite{haroche_exploring_2006}. As calculated in \refsec{w_er_hamiltonian}, the matrix elements of the ZQ and DQ transitions are very small compared to the EPR-allowed transitions. Therefore, the \textit{direct} shift is negligible.
    \item The NMR-allowed transitions are not significantly shifted as they are far too detuned with respect to the frequency of the drive.
\end{itemize}

Even if the direct shift of the ZQ and DQ transitions is negligible, the effect of the drive on the EPR-allowed transitions creates an effective shift on the ZQ and DQ transition frequencies, as shown in \reffig{ac_zeeman_diagram}. This shift is given by

\begin{equation}
\delta_{ZQ, DQ}(\Omega) = \mp \frac{1}{2} \left(\delta\omega^{(\uparrow)}_\mathrm{AC} + \delta\omega^{(\downarrow)}_\mathrm{AC}\right) \approx \mp \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}} + \frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}}\right).
\end{equation}

This approximation holds when the induced AC-Zeeman shifts are small compared to the intrinsic sideband detuning, ($|\delta_{ZQ,DQ}|\ll \omega_I$) so that the drive frequency remains effectively unchanged with respect to the unperturbed ZQ/DQ transitions.

\subsection{Cross-relaxation probability}
\labsec{cross_relaxation_half}

We now consider all possible relaxation mechanisms for the electron spin in the \W--\Er coupled system. Due to the coupling to the superconducting resonator, the main relaxation mechanism is spontaneous emission through the EPR-allowed transitions. The decay rate of this process is given by $\Gamma_\text{R}=4g_0^2/\kappa$ when the spin is in resonance with the cavity $\omega_S = \omega_r$ (see \refsec{spin_coupled_to_resonator}). In this event the state of the nuclear spin is preserved as these transitions only involve the electron spin. However, the small ZQ and DQ matrix elements allow the \Er spin to relax through any of these transitions, albeit with a much reduced decay rate. Through any of these cross-relaxation events, the state of both spins is simultaneously changed. Spontaneous emission through the ZQ and DQ transitions is also strongly enhanced by the Purcell effect. Their decay rate can be estimated by considering the Purcell effect on the ZQ and DQ transitions and rescaling by the relevant matrix element

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    &\Gamma_\text{ZQ} = \frac{g_0^2\kappa}{(\omega_r-\omega_\text{ZQ})^2 + \kappa^2/4} \, \frac{\bra{\Downarrow \uparrow}\hat{S}_x \ket{\Uparrow \downarrow}}{\bra{\Downarrow \downarrow}\hat{S}_x \ket{\Uparrow \downarrow}}, \\
    &\Gamma_\text{DQ} = \frac{g_0^2\kappa}{(\omega_r-\omega_\text{DQ})^2 + \kappa^2/4} \, \frac{\bra{\Downarrow \downarrow}\hat{S}_x \ket{\Uparrow \uparrow}}{\bra{\Downarrow \uparrow}\hat{S}_x \ket{\Uparrow \uparrow}}, \\
    &\Gamma_\text{DQ} \approx \Gamma_\text{DQ} \approx \frac{\Gamma_\text{R}}{1 + 4\omega_I^2 / \kappa^2} \, \frac{A_\perp^2}{4\omega_I^2}.
\end{split}
\end{align}

We define the probability of a cross-relaxation event after the electron spin has been excited as

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cross_relaxation_probability_half}
    p_\text{ZQ, DQ} = \frac{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ}}{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ} + \Gamma_\text{R}} \approx  \frac{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ}}{\Gamma_\text{R}} = \frac{1}{1 + 4\omega_I^2 / \kappa^2} \, \frac{A_\perp^2}{4\omega_I^2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stimulated Raman driving}
\labsec{raman_driving}

As discussed in the previous section, a pulse resonant with a ZQ/DQ transition can be used to coherently drive the nuclear spin state. As explained earlier, ZQ/DQ driving is not an efficient way to coherently drive a nuclear spin in our setup. In this section, we explain how a nuclear spin can be driven by the simultaneous application of two off-resonant microwave tones. This so-called Raman scheme is commonly used in optics to drive hyperfine transition in atoms \sidecite{kasevich_atomic_1991}, and we transpose it here to spin systems at microwave frequency. In this section, we derive the effective nuclear spin driving strength (the so-called Raman Rabi frequency $\Omega_\mathrm{Ram}$). We also derive formulas for the differential AC-Zeeman shifts undergone by the nuclear spin levels in presence of the Raman drives, which will be useful in Chapter \ref{ch:individual_nuclear_spins_quantum_information} to construct a two-qubit gate.

As a starting point, we consider a three-level system with a $\Lambda$-structure: two uncoupled ground states (named $\ket{a}$ \& $\ket{b}$) and an excited state (named $\ket{c}$), as shown in \reffig{raman_scheme}a. The two ground-to-excited transitions are off-resonantly driven by microwave tones with Rabi rates $\Omega_\mathrm{A}$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{B}$. The frequency difference between the $\ket{a} \leftrightarrow \ket{c}$ transition and drive A is given by $\Delta$ and the detuning between the two drives by $\delta$. In the dispersive regime ($|\Delta| \gg |\Omega_\mathrm{A, B}|$) the excited state can be adiabatically eliminated \sidecite{cohen-tannoudji_atom_2024}. When $\delta$ is resonant with the NMR-allowed transition frequency, the nuclear spin population is driven with Rabi frequency given by

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_\mathrm{Ram} = \frac{\Omega_\mathrm{A}\Omega_\mathrm{B}}{2\Delta}.
\end{equation}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/raman_scheme.pdf}
    \caption[Stimulated Raman Scheme]{Stimulated Raman Scheme. The two microwave pulses have amplitudes $\Omega_\text{A, B}$ the frequency difference between the two pulses is defined as $\delta$
    \textbf{a.} $\Lambda$-level system. $\Delta$ is defined as the detuning between drive A and the $\ket{a} \leftrightarrow \ket{c}$ transition.
    \textbf{b.} \W--\Er coupled system.  $\Delta$ is defined as the detuning between drive A and the electron spin transition when the nuclear spin is in state $\uparrow$.}
    \labfig{raman_scheme}
\end{marginfigure}

We now apply the Raman drive concept to the coupled electron-nuclear spin system. The level diagram in \reffig{raman_scheme}b now displays two $\Lambda$-systems, instead of a single one. In addition, the cavity filters the microwave amplitude as

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \Omega_\text{A} = \frac{\Omega_{e}}{\alpha_\text{A}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r-(\omega_S^{(\uparrow)}-\Delta))^2}{\kappa^2}}}, \\
    \Omega_\text{B} = \frac{\Omega_{e}}{\alpha_\text{B}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r-(\omega_S^{(\uparrow)}-\Delta-\delta))^2}{\kappa^2}}},
\end{split}
\label{raman_amplitudes}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_\text{A,B}$ is a linear scaling factor to account for differences in pulse amplitudes. Because each Raman pulse can couple the ground states to either of two excited states, $\ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}$ or $\ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}$ (see \reffig{raman_scheme}), the stimulated Raman transition is mediated by both paths. The total Rabi rate results from the combination of the independent rates, rescaled by the corresponding relative matrix elements,

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}    
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} =&\; \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},1} +\Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},2}  \approx -\frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{2\Delta} \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I} + \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{2(\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)})} \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:omega_raman}
\end{equation}

Because the ZQ and DQ matrix elements have opposite signs (Eq. \ref{eq:zq_dq_matrix_elements}), the resulting Raman Rabi rates necessarily carry opposite signs as well. The sign difference can be compensating with by driving "in the middle" of the two excited state levels, as the sign of the denominator will also be opposite in these conditions. The optimal point corresponds to $\Delta=-\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}/2$, since it results in the maximal frequency detuning of the drives with respect to the EPR-allowed transitions. This allows to increase their microwave amplitude without breaking the Raman condition $\Omega_\text{A,B} \ll \Delta$ which would significantly populate the \Er excited state. The Raman Rabi frequency at this condition is given by

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/raman_rabi_vs_detuning.pdf}
    \caption[Raman Rabi frequency as a function of $\Delta$]{Raman Rabi frequency as a function of $\Delta$. Data in blue is calculated with Eq.~\ref{eq:omega_raman} by considering $\alpha_\text{A,B} = 1$, $\Omega/2\pi = 200$~kHz and $A_\perp/2\pi=50$~kHz. Dashed vertical line marks $\Delta=-\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}/2$. The red shaded area indicates the hard limit beyond which the Raman approximation breaks down, i.e., $\Omega_{\mathrm{A,B}} \gtrsim \Delta$.}
    \labfig{raman_rabi_vs_detuning}
\end{marginfigure}

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} = \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}} \frac{A_\perp}{\omega_I} \approx \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B A_\perp}{\omega_I^2}
\end{equation}

\reffig{raman_rabi_vs_detuning} shows $\Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}}$ as a function of $\Delta$ for $\alpha_\text{A,B} = 1$,  $\Omega/2\pi = 200$~kHz and $A_\perp/2\pi=50$~kHz, which are close to experimental conditions.


\paragraph{Differential AC-Zeeman shift}

As described in the previous section, the presence of an off-resonance microwave drive renormalizes the energy levels of the system and induces an AC-Zeeman shift on the EPR-allowed transitions. In turn, this effectively shifts the resonance of the ZQ/DQ transition. Similarly, the two drives used for the stimulated Raman driving will renormalize the energy levels and create an effective shift $\delta_\text{AC}$ of the NMR transition which modifies the resonant condition of the stimulated Raman drive to $\delta = \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)} + \delta_\text{AC}$. The shift can be by considering the independent effect of each drive in each EPR-allowed transition,

\begin{equation}
    \delta_\text{AC} = \frac{\Omega_A^2}{4} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\Delta} - \frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)} -  \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}}\right) + \frac{\Omega_B^2}{4} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}} - \frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}}\right).
    \label{eq:ac_zeeman}
\end{equation}

In this equation, the first term corresponds to the shift of the A drive to the EPR-allowed transition when the nuclear spin is in $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ respectively and the second term corresponds to the shift of the B drive on the same electron transitions.

\section{High-spin generalization}
\labsec{high_spin_generalization}

In Chapter \ref{ch:nb_nuclear_spin} we experimentally identify a strongly coupled spin $I=9/2$, which we spectroscopically identify as \Nb. Our goal is to derive analytical formulas for the sideband driving, cross-relaxation rates, and Raman Rabi frequencies between pairs of nuclear spin levels $n$, $n+1$. These results will be used in Chapter \ref{ch:nb_nuclear_spin} to analyze the results and extract the hyperfine parameters. As discussed in \refsec{nb_er_hamiltonian}, the main differences between the two systems are the larger Hilbert space, which spans twenty energy levels, and the introduction of the nuclear-quadrupole interaction, which couples the nuclear spin to the electric field gradient at the \Nb position. 

\subsection{Zero- and double-quantum transitions}
\labsec{zero_double_quantum_ninehalf}

As derived in \refsec{nb_er_hamiltonian}, the Hamiltonian of the system is given by

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
       \cH{}/\hbar &= \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x + \\ 
        &+ \frac{\omega_Q'}{6} \left[3\hat{I}_{z}^2 + \frac{\eta'}{2}(e^{-2i\alpha}\hat{I}_+^2-e^{2i\alpha}\hat{I}_{-}^2)\right].
\end{split}
\end{align}

In the secular approximation, the electron wavefunctions are well described by the magnetic number $m_S$. However, the nuclear spin states are mixed to varying degree due to the hyperfine and the electric quadrupole interaction. In order to calculate the relevant matrix element we will treat the wavefunctions of the Hamiltonian in first-order perturbation theory. We consider as a perturbation $\hat{V}$ all terms that do not commute with $\hat{S}_z$ and $\hat{I}_z$, that is, 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
        \cH{} &= \cH{0}+\hat{V} \\
        \cH{0} &= \omega_S\cdot \hat{S}_z + \omega_I\cdot \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z +  \omega_Q'/2\cdot \hat{I}_z^2 \\
         \hat{V} & = A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x + \frac{\omega_Q'\eta'}{12}\,(e^{-2i\alpha}\hat{I}_+^2-e^{2i\alpha}\hat{I}_{-}^2),
\end{split}
\end{align}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/energy_levels_ninehalf.pdf}
    \caption[Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-9/2]{Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-9/2}
    \labfig{energy_levels_ninehalf}
\end{marginfigure}


The eigenstates of the unperturbed system $\cH{0}$ correspond to $\ket{m_S m_I}$, where $m_I \in \{-9/2,\dots,9/2\}$ are the nuclear spin quantum numbers. At first order, the hyperfine and quadrupole perturbations will mix terms with $m_I \pm 1$ and $m_I \pm 2$ respectively and the wavefunctions are given by

\begin{equation}
\label{seq:first-order-wfn}
    \ket{\psi_{m_S,m_I}} \approx \ket{m_S, m_I} + \sum_{i\in\{\pm1\pm2\}} M_i^{(m_S, m_I)} \cdot \ket{m_S, m_I + i}
\end{equation}

where 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
\label{eq:mixing_coefficients}
    &M_{\pm1}^{(m_S,m_I)} =  \mp\frac{C^\pm_{m_I} \cdot A_\perp m_S / 2}{\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S + \omega'_Q (m_I\pm1/2)}, \\
    &M_{\pm2}^{(m_S,m_I)} = \mp\frac{e^{\mp2i\alpha}D^\pm_{m_I} \cdot \omega_Q'\eta'/12}{2(\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S + \omega'_Q (m_I\pm1))}.\\ \\
    &C^\pm_{m_I} = \bra{m_I\pm1}\hat{I}_\pm\ket{m_I} = \sqrt{I(I+1)-m_I(m_I\pm1)},\\
    &D^\pm_{m_I} = \bra{m_I\pm2}\hat{I}_\pm\ket{m_I} = C^\pm_{m_I}~\cdot~C^\pm_{m_I+1}\,.
\end{split}
\end{align}

    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/ninehalf_matrix_elements.pdf}
    \caption[Spin ladder operators for $I=9/2$]{Spin ladder operators for a spin $I=9/2$ as a function of the spin magnetic number $m_I$. We note that $C^+_{m_I} = C^-_{m_I+1}$ and $D^+_{m_I} = D^-_{m_I+2}$.}
    \labfig{ninehalf_matrix_elements}
\end{marginfigure}

Due to the hyperfine and quadrupole induced mixing, the nuclear spin magnetic numbers are not good quantum numbers anymore, and we introduce the notation $\ket{m_S, n} = \ket{\psi_{m_S, 9/2-m_I}}$ with $n\in\{0,\dots,9\}$ in ascending order of energy. Due to the admixing of the nuclear spin wavefunctions, the matrix element for the EPR-allowed and ZQ/DQ transitions are given by 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
\label{eq:matrix_elements_nine_half}
    &\bra{\downarrow n}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n} \approx 1/2\\
    &\bra{\downarrow n\pm1}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n} \approx \mp\frac{1}{4}
\frac{A_\perp C^\pm_{n}}{\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n\pm\tfrac12\right)}\\.
\end{split}
\end{align}

%We note that even if the mixing between $\Delta m_I=2$ wavefunctions is larger by an order of magnitude, the change in nuclear wavefunctions between $\ket{\uparrow n}$ and $\ket{\downarrow n}$ is mainly dominated by the $M_{\pm1}$ coefficients, due to the proportionality to $m_S$ and the corresponding change of sign between the two states. However, the matrix elements for $i=\pm2$ are non-negligible, specially for high $n$. This indicates that higher order sidebands are also weakly allowed in this system, enabled by the quadrupole non-diagonal elements.

We note that $\Delta n = \pm2$ matrix elements are non-zero which means that higher order sidebands driving is permitted thanks to the quadrupole mixing. In the weak-quadrupole regime these $\Delta m_I = \pm 2$ matrix elements are much smaller than their $\Delta m_I = \pm 2$ counterparts. Moreover, the $\Delta m_I = \pm 2$ lines are typically farther detuned from the resonator center, so cavity filtering further suppresses the effective drive amplitude. The net effect is that driving $\Delta m_I = \pm 2$ transitions requires higher microwave power with much slower Rabi rates, and we do not treat them in the following.

Analogous to the $I=1/2$ case, the nuclear population of the $I=9/2$ system can be addressed by resonantly driving the weakly allowed sideband transitions. The perpendicular hyperfine term is responsible for the dominant $\Delta m_I = \pm 1$ ZQ and DQ sidebands, while the non-diagonal quadrupole terms open additional $\Delta m_I = \pm 2$ paths. 

We arbitrarily consider the ZQ transitions from state $n$ to $n+1$ with frequency $\omega_n^{(+1)}$, but the following derivation is analogous for the DQ transitions. As in the previous section, the oscillation frequency $\Omega^{(n)}_{\text{ZQ}}$ depends on two factors: the matrix element of the transition and the amplitude of the applied microwave field, quantified by an electron Rabi frequency $\Omega$ measured at a reference pulse amplitude. Using $\Omega$ as a calibration point, we express the drive amplitude for any pulse as a relative factor $\alpha$. The corresponding sideband Rabi frequency is then 

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \Omega^{(n)}_{\text{ZQ}} =\; \frac{\Omega}{\alpha} \, \frac{A_\perp\, C^+_{n}}{2\,\left(\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n+\tfrac12\right)\right)}\, \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r-\omega_n^{(+1)})^2}{\kappa^2}} } 
\end{split}
\label{eq:zero_double_quantum_ninehalf_rabi}
\end{equation}

As for the case of the nuclear-spin $I=1/2$, the oscillation frequency is proportional to the coupling strength. However, the ladder factors $C^+_{n}$ will modulate the driving speed depending on the state of the nuclear spin, with the highest factor for the $n=4$ to $n=5$ transition which approximately corresponds to the $m_I=1/2$ to $m_I=-1/2$ transition (see \reffig{ninehalf_matrix_elements}). In addition, when driving the ZQ and DQ transitions, the energy levels will experience an AC-Zeeman shift, as described in the spin-1/2 case.

\subsection{Cross-relaxation rates}

Using the calculated matrix elements we now proceed to give an estimation of the relaxation rate of the electron spin through all the available mechanisms. The main relaxation channel for the electron spin comes from the EPR-allowed transitions, which leave the nuclear spin invariant, and the matrix element for these transitions is approximately 1/2 no matter the state of the nuclear spin. However, the hyperfine interaction shifts the frequency of these transitions ($\omega_0, \dots,\omega_9$) depending on the state of the nuclear spin. Experimentally, we tune the spin such that $\omega_0=\omega_r$ which results in different Purcell factors for every transition. The electron relaxation rate when the nuclear spin is in state $n$, $\Gamma_R^{(n)}$, is given by

\begin{equation}
    \Gamma^{(n)}_R = \frac{\kappa g_0^2}{\kappa^2/4 + (\omega_r - \omega_n)^2} =  \Gamma^{(0)}_R \cdot \frac{1}{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r - \omega_n)^2}{\kappa^2}}
\end{equation}


where $\Gamma^{(0)}_R$ is the electron decay rate when $n=0$. We denote the frequency for a cross-relaxation where the state of the nuclear spin is changed from $n$ to $n+i$ as $\omega_{n}^{(i)}$. The decay rate is obtained by rescaling the relaxation rate given by the Purcell effect by the matrix element
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \Gamma^{(n)}_{i} =&\, \frac{\kappa g_0^2}{\kappa^2/4 + (\omega_r - \omega_n^{(i)})^2} \cdot \frac{|\bra{\downarrow n+i}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n}|^2}{|\bra{\downarrow n}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n}|^2} = \\ 
    =&\, 2\,\Gamma^{(0)}_R \cdot \frac{|\bra{\downarrow n+i}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n}|^2}{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r - \omega_{n}^{(i)})^2}{\kappa^2}}
\end{split}
\end{align}

The total relaxation rate for $\ket{\uparrow n}$ is given by $\Gamma^{(n)} = \Gamma^{(n)}_R +\sum_i\Gamma^{(n)}_i$. We naturally introduce the cross-relaxation probability

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cross_relaxation_probability_ninehalf}
    p^{(n)}_{x,i} = \frac{\Gamma^{(n)}_i}{\Gamma^{(n)}} \approx \frac{\Gamma^{(n)}_i}{\Gamma^{(n)}_R} = \frac{2|\bra{\downarrow n+i}\hat{S}_x\ket{\uparrow n}|^2}{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r - \omega^{(n)}_i)^2}{\kappa^2}}.
\end{equation}

Due to the difference in scale of the matrix elements between the $\Delta m_I \pm 1$ and $\Delta m_I \pm 2$ transitions, the dominant contribution to the relaxation dynamics arises from the ZQ and DQ transitions, while the probability of a cross-relaxation event with $\Delta m_I = \pm2$ is orders of magnitude weaker.


\subsection{Stimulated Raman driving}
\labsec{stimulated_raman_driving_ninehalf}

In this section we derive the expressions for the Rabi frequency of a Raman drive for the case of a nuclear-spin $I=9/2$ by generalizing the results obtained in the previous section of a spin $I=1/2$. As in this previous section, the stimulated Raman scheme allows to transfer the population between the two adjacent nuclear spin levels $\ket{\Downarrow n}$ and $\ket{\Downarrow n+1}$ through the use of two off-resonant microwave drives with amplitudes $\Omega_\text{A}$ and $\Omega_\text{B}$ (see Eq. \ref{raman_amplitudes}). When the difference in frequency between the two drives matches the nuclear spin transition frequency, a two-photon process enables the coherent control of the nuclear spin population between the two levels. This is possible by leveraging the coupling of the two levels of interest to their electron excited counterparts: $\ket{\Uparrow n}$ and $\ket{\Uparrow n+1}$. As in the previous section, the presence of two electron excited states creates two possible paths for the scheme, which interfere with each other,

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}    
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} =&\; \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},1} +\Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},2} \\ \\
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},1} \approx&\; -\frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{4\Delta} \frac{A_\perp C^+_{n}}{\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n+\tfrac12\right)} \\ \\
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},2} \approx&\; + \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{4\left(\Delta + \omega_n\right)} \frac{A_\perp C^+_{n}}{\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n+\tfrac12\right)},
\end{split}
\end{equation}

where we have used that $C^+_{m_I} = C^-_{m_I+1}$. As in the case of $I=1/2$, the matrix elements have opposite signs and constructive interference is only achieved while driving between the two excited state levels. Similarly, the optimal driving point is given for $\Delta=-\omega_n/2$, as it maximizes the detuning between the drives and the EPR-allowed transitions. The Raman Rabi frequency in this regime is given by

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} = \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{\omega_n} \frac{A_\perp C^+_{n}}{\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n+\tfrac12\right)} \approx A_\perp C^+_{n}\frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{\omega_n^2}
    \label{eq:stimulated_raman_ninehalf_aperp}
\end{equation}

We note that the final value of $\Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}}$ is the same as in the $I=1/2$ case, but scaled by the ladder-operator factor $C^+_n$.

\section{Summary}
\labsec{summary_spin_driving}

This chapter has established the theoretical framework for nuclear spin control mediated by an electron spin using microwave drives. We have discussed two mechanisms, resonant driving of the ZQ/DQ transition and microwave stimulated Raman driving. The discussion was separated into two parts: first, the case of nuclear spins $I=1/2$, which will be used in Chapters \ref{ch:detection_and_control_of_individual_w} and \ref{ch:individual_nuclear_spins_quantum_information}, and second, the generalization to high-spin nuclei ($I>1/2$) used in Chapter \ref{ch:nb_nuclear_spin}.  

\paragraph{Zero- and double-quantum transitions.} Direct driving of the ZQ/DQ transitions allows simultaneous control of the electron and nuclear spin state. The matrix element of the transition is small compared to the EPR-allowed transitions and large microwave amplitude pulses are required to effectively drive Rabi oscillations. The use of large microwave amplitudes induces AC-Zeeman shifts of the EPR-allowed transitions which translates to effective shifts of the ZQ and DQ transition frequencies. However, Even at large microwave amplitudes the scheme is limited by the electron spin decoherence since the excited state is populated by the drive.

\paragraph{Cross-relaxation events.} Due to the non-zero ZQ and DQ matrix elements, the electron spin can spontaneously decay through a ZQ or DQ transition, changing the state of the nuclear spin in the process. The probability for this cross-relaxation event scales proportionally to the square of the perpendicular hyperfine coupling, $A_\perp$.

\paragraph{Stimulated Raman driving.} Control of the nuclear spin can be achieved by applying two off-resonant microwave tones with a mutual detuning equal to the NMR-allowed transition frequency~\sidecite{cohen-tannoudji_atom_2024}. This microwave stimulated Raman scheme does not significantly populate the electron spin excited state and preserves the coherence time of nuclear spins. The two microwave drives also renormalize the energy levels of the system which result in a \textit{differential} AC-Zeeman shift of the NMR-allowed transition.
