\chapter{Conclusion and future directions}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section*{Summary}

The objective of this thesis has been to demonstrate an all-microwave platform for the detection of nuclear spins in the solid state using an electronic spin as an ancilla, and to show how this system can be used for quantum information and precision spectroscopy.

In Chapter \ref{ch:detection_and_control_of_individual_w}, we establish the basis for nuclear spin detection by monitoring the fluorescence of a strongly coupled electronic spin. This demonstration relies on several key technologies. First, the Single Microwave Photon Detector provides the sensitivity required to count individual photons in the microwave regime. Second, Purcell enhancement of radiative spin transitions of \Er ions in \Ca boosts the microwave emission beyond the phonon relaxation rate. By combining these two technologies, we \red{XX}. Using high-resolution EPR spectroscopy, we resolve the hyperfine splitting of the \Er spin coupled to a neighboring \W. We exploit the frequency differences of the EPR transitions, which depend on the \W nuclear spin state, to define a nuclear spin readout protocol. In addition, we demonstrate solid-effect dynamical nuclear polarization of the nuclear spin environment surrounding the \Er.

Chapter \ref{ch:individual_nuclear_spins_quantum_information} builds on these techniques to construct a two-qubit register composed of two strongly coupled \W nuclear spins. We demonstrate coherent control using only microwave tones through a stimulated Raman scheme and define single- and two-qubit gates by exploiting microwave-induced energy level shifts. We show that, owing to the low magnetic noise environment of \Ca, nuclear spins achieve coherence times on the order of seconds. These coherence times can be further extended by preparing multi-qubit states that are inherently protected from magnetic noise.

Finally, in Chapter \ref{ch:nb_nuclear_spin}, we chemically and structurally identify a \Nb impurity neighboring an \Er spin using the techniques developed in the previous chapters. Leveraging the long nuclear spin coherence, we measure the NMR transitions with hertz-level precision and fit them to a Hamiltonian model. We observe a significant shift in the nuclear quadrupole interaction depending on the electronic spin state, which we attribute to a new type of electrostatic interaction between electronic and nuclear spins in the solid state. This interaction originates from the magnetically induced electric dipole of the \Er spin and is mediated by the crystal lattice. Finally, we use high-resolution data to place an upper bound on the hexadecapole interaction of the \Nb that is an order of magnitude tighter than previous solid-state NMR results.
