\section{Driving a nuclear spin-1/2}
\labsec{driving_nuclear_spin_half}

If one wishes to drive the state of a nuclear spin, the most straightforward way is to apply a radiofrequency (rf) pulse resonant with the allowed NMR transitions. However, the superconducting resonator in our setup acts as a narrow band-pass filter centered around $\omega_r\approx7.7$~GHz, as detailed in \refsec{rabi_oscillations_theory}. Consequently, all rf drives applied to the sample will be reflected from the resonator and direct driving of a nuclear spin becomes impossible.

For \W at a field of 0.45~T, the ZQ and DQ transitions are detuned from the electron spin transition $\sim$800~kHz; within the resonator linewidth $\kappa$. Therefore, when the resonator is tuned to the electron spin, the ZQ and DQ transitions can be excited through a microwave tone. By driving these transitions, one can coherently control the state of the electron and the nuclear spin simultaneously. We now proceed to derive the Rabi rate of ZQ and DQ transitions via direct driving. We begin by recalling the Hamiltonian and energy level system derived in \refsec{w_er_hamiltonian}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/energy_levels_half.pdf}
    \caption[Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2]{\textbf{a.} Energy level diagram of an \Er coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2. Electron spin transitions are marked with a black arrow while zero and double quantum transitions are marked with blue and red arrows respectively.
    \textbf{b.} Schematic of the transitions, superimposed with the resonator response shown as a gray Lorentzian of FWHM$\kappa$. Allowed transitions (black peaks) are quasi-resonant with $\omega_r$, forbidden transitions (red and blue) are detuned by $\sim \omega_I$}
    \labfig{energy_levels_half}
\end{marginfigure}

\begin{equation}
    \cH{}/\hbar = \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x.
\end{equation}

which results in the four energy levels shown in \reffig{energy_levels_half}a which approximately correspond to the unperturbed spin states in the high-field regime:

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \ket{1} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}     + \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}\right) , \\
    \ket{2} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}   - \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}\right), \\
    \ket{3} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Downarrow\uparrow}   - \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Downarrow\downarrow}\right) , \\
    \ket{4} &\approx \tfrac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left(\ket{\Downarrow\downarrow} + \tfrac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ket{\Downarrow\uparrow}\right),
\end{split}
\end{align}


where $\mathcal{N}$ is a normalization constant.

The perturbed states produce non-zero, though small, matrix elements between the mixed states. From the first-order wavefunctions we find

\begin{equation}
    \bra{1} S_x \ket{4} \approx - \bra{2} S_x \ket{3} \approx \frac{A_\perp}{4\omega_I} \ll 1.
    \label{eq:zq_dq_matrix_elements}
\end{equation}

so the zero- and double-quantum transitions are weakly allowed, but with a much smaller oscillator strength than the allowed EPR transitions

\begin{equation}
    \bra{1} S_x \ket{3} \approx \bra{2} S_x \ket{4} \approx 1/2,
\end{equation}

The Rabi rate of an allowed EPR transition with a resonant the cavity is given in \refsec{rabi_oscillations_theory} by $\Omega_d = \frac{8 g_0 \epsilon_d^2}{\kappa^2}$, where $\epsilon_d$ is the applied microwave amplitude. To obtain the ZQ and DQ Rabi rates at the \textit{same} drive amplitude we simply rescale $\Omega_d$ by the ratio of the relevant matrix elements. Including the cavity filtering (the sideband is detuned from the cavity by $\sim\omega_I$) gives the final ZQ/DQ Rabi rate

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\text{ZQ}} \approx \Omega_{\text{DQ}} \approx \Omega_d \cdot \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4\omega_I^2/\kappa^2}}.
\end{equation}

Because the matrix elements and the cavity filtering are nearly the same for the ZQ and DQ sidebands, their driven Rabi rates are essentially equal. However, sideband driving necessarily involves the electron spin, so the operation is limited by the much faster electron dephasing time $T_2^*$ rather than by the long nuclear coherence. Consequently, electron spin decoherence rate imposes a fundamental limit on the fidelity of nuclear-spin gates implemented via sideband drives.

\subsection{AC-Zeeman shifts}
\labsec{ac-zeeman_shifts}

We now introduce the concept of AC-Zeeman shifts, also known as the magnetic AC-Stark shift. This effect is derived by treating the coherent drive as a time-dependent perturbation and using second-order perturbation theory \sidecite{cohen-tannoudji_atom_2024}. For a single electron transition driven with Rabi rate $\Omega$ and detuning $\Delta$ (defined the difference between the transition and the drive frequencies) the drive produces an energy shift of the two levels so that the driven transition frequency is shifted by

\begin{equation}
    \delta\omega_\mathrm{AC} = \frac{\Omega^2}{2\Delta}.
\end{equation}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/ac_zeeman_diagram.pdf}
    \caption[AC Zeeman shift diagram]{AC Zeeman shift diagram. Driving the ZQ transition induces AC-Zeeman shifts on the electron spin transitions ($\delta\omega^{(\uparrow)}_\mathrm{AC}$, $\delta\omega^{(\downarrow)}_\mathrm{AC}$), which modify the resonance condition for the sideband transition.}
    \labfig{ac_zeeman_diagram}
\end{marginfigure}

In the four-level system considered here, any non-resonant microwave drive produces AC-Zeeman shifts on transitions with nonzero oscillator strength. Because the electron spin transitions carry the dominant oscillator strength, we neglect the direct AC-Zeeman shifts of the ZQ and DQ sidebands. Nevertheless, the shifts of the electron levels change the energies of the states involved and therefore modify the effective resonance conditions of the ZQ and DQ transitions. We can quantify this effect by computing the AC-Zeeman shifts induced on the two electron spin transitions and summing their net contribution to the sideband detunings (see \reffig{ac_zeeman_diagram}):

\begin{equation}
\delta_{ZQ, DQ}(\Omega) = \mp \frac{1}{2} \left(\delta\omega^{(\uparrow)}_\mathrm{AC} + \delta\omega^{(\downarrow)}_\mathrm{AC}\right) \approx \mp \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}} + \frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}}\right).
\end{equation}

This approximation holds when the induced AC-Zeeman shifts are small compared to the intrinsic sideband detuning, ($|\delta_{ZQ,DQ}|\ll \omega_I$) so that the drive frequency remains effectively unchanged with respect to the unperturbed ZQ/DQ transitions.

\subsection{Cross-relaxation probability}
\labsec{cross_relaxation_half}

The main relaxation channel for the electron in the excited state is the Purcell enhanced electron spin transition, which leave the nuclear spin state invariant. These transitions have an oscillator strength of approximately 1/2, no matter the nuclear spin state, but two electron spin transitions have slightly different frequencies $\omega_S^{(\uparrow, \downarrow)}$. However, since the resonator linewidth is much larger than the frequency difference between the two transitions ($\kappa \gg A_\parallel$), the Purcell enhancement for both transitions is approximately equal and given by Equation \ref{eq:Purcell_formula}. Experimentally, we tune the electron spin Larmor frequency close to the resonator frequency (see \reffig{energy_levels_half}b), and the relaxation rate is given by $\Gamma_\text{S}=4g_0^2/\kappa$.

However, the mixing of the nuclear spin wavefunctions also creates a relaxation path through the zero and double-quantum transitions with a much smaller oscillator strength. The relaxation rate can be calculated by considering the Purcell enhancement of the transition and rescaling by the relative oscillator strength,

\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_\text{ZQ} \approx \Gamma_\text{DQ} \approx \frac{\Gamma_\text{S}}{1 + 4\omega_I^2 / \kappa^2} \, \frac{A_\perp^2}{2\omega_I^2}.
\end{equation}

Where we have assumed that the spin is tuned to the cavity ($\omega_r = \omega_S$). Finally, using these rates we define the probability of a cross-relaxation event after the electron spin has been excited

\begin{equation}
    p_\text{ZQ, DQ} = \frac{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ}}{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ} + \Gamma_\text{S}} \approx  \frac{\Gamma_\text{ZQ, DQ}}{\Gamma_\text{S}} = \frac{1}{1 + 4\omega_I^2 / \kappa^2} \, \frac{A_\perp^2}{2\omega_I^2}.
\end{equation}

\section{Stimulated Raman driving}
\labsec{raman_driving}

As discussed in the previous section, a pulse resonant with a sideband transition can be used to coherently drive the nuclear spin state. This driving scheme is mainly limited by the electron spin coherence as the drive involves both the electron and nuclear spin. In addition, the ZQ and DQ transition have small oscillator strength and need a microwave large amplitude to achieve driving Rabi rates faster than the electron spin coherence $T_2^*$. Consequently, sideband driving is not the appropriate scheme to perform high fidelity nuclear spin manipulation. In this section we introduce stimulated Raman driving, a two-photon process used to coherently control the state of the nuclear spin without populating the electron spin excited state. 

As a starting point, we consider a three-level system with a $\Lambda$-structure: two uncoupled ground states and an excited state. The two ground-to-excited transitions are driven by microwave tones with Rabi rates $\Omega_\mathrm{A}$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{B}$. The frequency difference between the one of the drives and the transition is given by $\Delta$ and the detuning between the two drives by $\delta$. In the dispersive regime ($|\Delta| \gg |\Omega_\mathrm{A, B}|$) the excited state can be adiabatically eliminated \sidecite{cohen-tannoudji_atom_2024} and when $\delta$ matches the frequency difference between the two ground states there is a coherent drive with Rabi rate 

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_\mathrm{Ram} = \frac{\Omega_\mathrm{A}\Omega_\mathrm{B}}{2\Delta}.
\end{equation}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/raman_scheme.pdf}
    \caption[Stimulated Raman Scheme]{Stimulated Raman Scheme. To microwave pulses with amplitude $\Omega_\text{A, B}$ are applied on a four-level system. The detuning between drive A and the electron spin transition when the nuclear spin is in state $\uparrow$ is defined as $\Delta$ and the frequency difference between the two pulses is defined as $\delta$}
    \labfig{raman_scheme}
\end{marginfigure}

We now take the system of an electron spin coupled to a nuclear spin-1/2 described in the previous section. Compared to the $\Lambda$-system, there is an extra excited level, simultaneously coupled to the two ground states. In addition, the cavity filters the microwave amplitude as

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \Omega_\text{A} = \frac{\Omega_{e}}{\alpha_\text{A}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r-(\omega_S^{(\uparrow)}-\Delta))^2}{\kappa^2}}}, \\
    \Omega_\text{B} = \frac{\Omega_{e}}{\alpha_\text{B}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4\frac{(\omega_r-(\omega_S^{(\uparrow)}-\Delta-\delta))^2}{\kappa^2}}},
\end{split}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_\text{A,B}$ is a linear scaling factor to account for differences in pulse amplitudes. Because each Raman pulse can couple the ground states to either of two excited states, $\ket{\Uparrow\uparrow}$ or $\ket{\Uparrow\downarrow}$ (see \reffig{raman_scheme}), the stimulated Raman transition is mediated by both paths. The total Rabi rate results from the combination of the independent rates, rescaled by the corresponding relative matrix elements,

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}    
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} =&\; \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},1} +\Omega_{\mathrm{Ram},2}  \approx -\frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{2\Delta} \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I} + \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{2(\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)})} \frac{A_\perp}{2\omega_I}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

Because the ZQ and DQ matrix elements have opposite signs (Eq. \ref{eq:zq_dq_matrix_elements}), the resulting Raman Rabi rates necessarily carry opposite signs as well. Consequently, the maximum value for the total Rabi frequency $\Omega_{\text{Ram}}$ is given for $\Delta=-\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}/2$, as driving in between the two excited states compensates the sign difference, resulting in

\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\mathrm{Ram}} = \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B}{\omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}} \frac{A_\perp}{\omega_I} \approx \frac{\Omega_A\Omega_B A_\perp}{\omega_I^2}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Differential AC-Zeeman shift}

The two stimulated Raman drives are detuned with respect to the electron spin transitions, which hold most of the oscillator strength. As discussed in \refsec{ac-zeeman_shifts}, this will induce an AC-Zeeman shift on the energy levels of the system. The combination of shifts will modify the nuclear spin transition frequency and effectively change the resonant condition for the stimulated Raman transition to $\delta = \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)} + \delta_\text{AC}$. The shift can be directly calculated as the combined shift of each drive on every electron transition,

\begin{equation}
    \delta_\text{AC} = \frac{\Omega_A^2}{4} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\Delta} - \frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)} -  \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}}\right) + \frac{\Omega_B^2}{4} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Downarrow)}} - \frac{1}{\Delta + \omega_I^{(\Uparrow)}}\right).
\end{equation}

In this equation, the first term corresponds to the shift of the A drive to the electron spin transition when the nuclear spin is in $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ respectively and the second term corresponds to the shift of the B drive on the same electron transitions. As before, we disregard any shifts on the ZQ and DQ transitions as the oscillator strength is much smaller.

    \cH{}/\hbar = \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x + \hat{\mathbf{I}} \cdot \bar{\bar{Q}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{I}}.

In the basis of the principal axis of the quadrupole tensor ($\mathcal{P}_1$, $\mathcal{P}_2$, $\mathcal{P}_3$) the quadrupole Hamiltonian takes the following form (see \refsec{electric_quadrupole}),

\begin{equation}
    \cH{Q}/\hbar = \frac{\omega_Q}{6}\left[ 3 \hat{I}_{\mathcal{P}_1}^2 - I(I+1) + \eta(\hat{I}_{\mathcal{P}_2}^2 - \hat{I}_{\mathcal{P}_3}^2)\right],

We note that the principal axes of the quadrupole tensor need not coincide with the nuclear-spin operator axes\sidenote{In the nuclear-spin operator frame the $z$-axis is defined by the external field $B_0$ (approximately parallel to the crystal $c$-axis). In \red{refch} we show that one of the principal axis of the quadrupole of the measured system ($\mathcal{P}_3$) lies along the crystalline $c$-axis. The other two axis lie in the $(a,b)$-plane and the $\mathcal{P}_1$ axis makes an angle $\alpha_Q$ with respect to the crystalline $a$-axis. Because $B_0$ is nearly parallel to $c$, we adopt the approximation $z \parallel c \parallel \mathcal{P}_3$. Under this approximation the quadrupole Hamiltonian can be written as

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
        \cH{Q}/\hbar &= \frac{\omega_Q'}{6} \left[3\hat{I}_{z}^2 + \frac{\eta'}{2}(e^{-2i\alpha}\hat{I}_+^2-e^{2i\alpha}\hat{I}_{-}^2)\right] + \text{cnt}, \\
        \omega_Q' &= -\frac{\eta+1}{2} \, \omega_Q, \\ 
        \eta' &= \frac{\eta-3}{1+\eta}.
\end{split}
\end{align} 

where $\alpha=\alpha_\text{hf} - \alpha_Q$. Therefore, the Hamiltonian of the system can be approximated by 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
       \cH{}/\hbar &= \omega_S \hat{S}_z + \omega_I \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z + A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x + \\ 
        &+ \frac{\omega_Q'}{6} \left[3\hat{I}_{z}^2 + \frac{\eta'}{2}(e^{-2i\alpha}\hat{I}_+^2-e^{2i\alpha}\hat{I}_{-}^2)\right].
\end{split}
\end{align}

We consider as a perturbation all terms that do not commute with $\hat{S}_z$ and $\hat{I}_z$, that is, the perpendicular hyperfine term $A_\perp \hat{S}_z\hat{I}_x$ and the non-diagonal quadrupole terms.

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
        \cH{} &= \cH{0}+\hat{V} \\
        \cH{0} &= \omega_S\cdot \hat{S}_z + \omega_I\cdot \hat{I}_z + A_\parallel \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_z +  \omega_Q'/2\cdot \hat{I}_z^2 \\
         \hat{V} & = A_\perp \hat{S}_z \hat{I}_x + \frac{\omega_Q'\eta'}{12}\,(e^{-2i\alpha}\hat{I}_+^2-e^{2i\alpha}\hat{I}_{-}^2),
\end{split}
\end{align}

\begin{marginfigure}
    \includegraphics{chapter2/figures/energy_levels_ninehalf.pdf}
    \caption[Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-9/2]{Energy level diagram of an Er3+ coupled to a nuclear spin-9/2}
    \labfig{energy_levels_ninehalf}
\end{marginfigure}


The eigenstates of the unperturbed system correspond to $\ket{m_s m_I}$, where $m_I \in \{-9/2,\dots,9/2\}$ are the nuclear spin quantum numbers. The hyperfine and quadrupole perturbations will mix terms with $m_I \pm 1$ and $m_I \pm 2$ respectively. At first order, the perturbed states will be

\begin{equation}
\label{seq:first-order-wfn}
    \ket{\psi_{m_S,m_I}} \approx \ket{m_S, m_I} + \sum_{i\in\{\pm1\pm2\}} M_i^{(m_S, m_I)} \cdot \ket{m_S, m_I + i}
\end{equation}

where 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
\label{seq:mixing_coefficients}
    &M_{\pm1}^{(m_S,m_I)} =  \mp\frac{C^\pm_{m_I} \cdot A_\perp m_S / 2}{\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S + \omega'_Q (m_I\pm1/2)}, \\
    &M_{\pm2}^{(m_S,m_I)} = \mp\frac{e^{\mp2i\alpha}D^\pm_{m_I} \cdot \omega_Q'\eta'/12}{2(\omega_I+A_\parallel m_S + \omega'_Q (m_I\pm1))}.\\ \\
    &C^\pm_{m_I} = \bra{m_I+1}I_\pm\ket{m_I} = \sqrt{I(I+1)-m_I(m_I\pm1)},\\
    &D^\pm_{m_I} = \bra{m_I+2}I_\pm\ket{m_I} = C^\pm_{m_I}~\cdot~C^\pm_{m_I+1}\,.
\end{split}
\end{align}

Given that the value of the coefficients is not negligible, the nuclear spin magnetic numbers are not good quantum numbers anymore, and we introduce the notation $\ket{m_S, n} = \ket{\psi_{m_s, 9/2-m_I}}$ with $n\in\{0,\dots,9\}$ in ascending order of energy. 

\reffig{energy_levels_ninehalf} sketches the energy level structure of the system as well as the electron and nuclear spin transitions. There are ten different electron spin transitions which depend on the state of the nuclear spin

\begin{equation}
    \omega_n \approx \omega_S + A_\parallel \, (n-9/2).
\end{equation}

In addition, there are eighteen single-quantum nuclear spin transitions labeled $\omega_{n(n+1)}^{(m_S)}$. Due to the admixing of the nuclear spin wavefunctions, the oscillator strength for the allowed and weakly allowed transitions are given by 

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    &\bra{\downarrow n}S_x\ket{\uparrow n} \approx 1/2\\
    &\bra{\downarrow n\pm1}S_x\ket{\uparrow n} \approx \mp\frac{1}{4}
\frac{A_\perp C^\pm_{n}}{\omega_I+\omega'_Q\!\left(n\pm\tfrac12\right)}\\
    &\bra{\downarrow n\pm2}S_x\ket{\uparrow n} \approx \mp \frac{\omega'_Q\eta'}{24}\,
\frac{A_\parallel\,e^{\mp2i\alpha}\,D^\pm_{n}}{2\left(\omega_I+\omega'_Q(n\pm1)\right)^2}
, \\[6pt]
\end{split}
\end{align}

We note that even if the mixing between $\Delta m_I=2$ wavefunctions is larger by an order of magnitude, the change in nuclear wavefunctions between $\ket{\uparrow n}$ and $\ket{\downarrow n}$ is mainly dominated by the $M_{\pm1}$ coefficients, due to the proportionality to $m_S$ and the corresponding change of sign between the two states. However, the matrix elements for $i=\pm2$ are non-negligible, specially for high $n$. This indicates that higher order sidebands are also weakly allowed in this system, enabled by the quadrupole non-diagonal elements.
