\chapter{Conclusion and future directions}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The objective of thesis has been the demonstration of an all-microwave setup for the detection of nuclear spins in the solid state using an electronic spin as an ancilla, as well as how to use this system for quantum information and precision spectroscopy. 

In Chapter \ref{ch:detection_and_control_of_individual_w} we set the basis for nuclear spin detection by monitoring the fluorescence of a strongly coupled electronic spin. This demonstration is enabled by several key technologies: first and foremost, the Single Microwave Photon Detector, which is the only available device with the sufficient sensitivity to count individual photons in the microwave regime. Second, the Purcell enhancement of radiative spin transition of \Er ions in \Ca, which allows to boost the microwave emission beyond the phonon relaxation rate. By combining these two technologies we \red{XX}. Using high-resolution EPR spectroscopy we were able to resolve the hyperfine splitting of the \Er spin coupled to a neighboring \W. We leverage the difference in frequency in the EPR transitions, which depends on the \W nuclear spin state, to define a readout protocol of the nuclear spin. Additionally, we demonstrated solid-effect dynamical nuclear polarization on the neighboring environment of the \Er.

Chapter \ref{ch:individual_nuclear_spins_quantum_information} builds upon these techniques to construct a two-qubit register composed of two strongly coupled \W nuclear spin. We demonstrate coherent control using only microwave tones through a stimulated Raman scheme, and we define single and two-qubit gates by exploiting the energy level shifts induced by the microwave pulses. We show that, thanks to the low magnetic environment of \Ca, nuclear spins can achieve seconds long coherence, which can be further extended by creating multi-qubit states that are inherently protected of magnetic noise.

Finally, in Chapter \ref{ch:nb_nuclear_spin} we chemically and structurally identify a \Nb impurity neighboring an \Er spin using the collection of techniques developed in the previous two chapters. We leverage the long coherence of the nuclear spin to measure the NMR transitions with hertz precision and fit it to a Hamiltonian model. We observe a significant shift in the nuclear quadrupole interaction depending on the state of the electron spin, which we associate with a new type of electrostatic interaction between electron and nuclear spins in the solid-state. This has its origin on the magnetically induced electric dipole of the \Er spin and is mediated by the crystal lattice. Finally, we use high-resolution data to set higher bound on the hexadecapole interaction of the \Nb, which are one order of magnitude tighter than previously achieved in solid-state NMR.
